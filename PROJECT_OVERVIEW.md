We investigated how to choose a speech-to-text model for searching recorded meetings, using a public Hugging Face leaderboard as our starting point. We tested three openly available models—Whisper large-v3, Whisper large-v3-turbo, and NVIDIA Parakeet—on the same 800 meeting-audio clips, totaling about 50 minutes. Parakeet made substantially fewer transcription errors in this sample; the smaller difference between the two Whisper models was less conclusive.

We then asked whether counting every incorrect word equally tells the whole story for meeting search. We tried treating related word forms, such as “button” and “buttons,” as equivalent, and giving extra importance to recurring meeting terminology. We developed those choices using separate meetings, then rescored the existing transcripts. The scores changed, but Parakeet remained the overall leader. These measures reflect possible search priorities; we have not yet tested whether they improve actual search results.

Finally, we examined how performance varied across meetings, speakers, meeting roles, and clip lengths, and packaged the code, results, and documentation for further work. The broader lesson is that a leaderboard score is a useful starting point, but model selection also depends on the recordings, the scoring rules, and what the application needs to preserve. The project also provides a worked example for assessing and scoping the proposed student exercise.
